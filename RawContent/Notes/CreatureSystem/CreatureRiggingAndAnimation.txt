**Creature Rigging & Animation Workflow**  
*Woodcraft Survival – UE 5.8 / Blender 5.2*  
*Tracking document for skeleton, rigging, shape keys, and animation process across all creatures*  
*(Created 2026-08-19 – living document, updated 2026-08-21)*

This document is the central reference for how creatures are rigged and prepared for animation.  
It covers general process, recommended baselines, facial/expression systems, Blender authoring IK, and records where species-specific decisions live.

It sits alongside:
- `CreatureAISystemDefinition.txt`
- `CreatureMaterialsAndAppearance.txt`

---

### 1. Goals

- Clean, maintainable skeletons suitable for a solo developer.
- Good deformation for locomotion, combat, and expression without excessive bone counts.
- Consistent hierarchy and naming so new creatures can reuse animation patterns and AnimBP logic where possible.
- Support both realistic movement needs and the project’s stylized (edge-shaded, flat color, polygonal) art direction.
- Shape keys + bones used pragmatically — prefer the cheapest solution that still reads clearly.
- Easy export path to Unreal Engine 5.8 (**deform bones only**; no Blender IK/controllers on the exported skeleton).

### 2. General Rigging Process

#### 2.1 High-Level Steps (Blender)

1. Place non-deforming **`root`** at ground stance center, then deforming **`pelvis`** at the hip mass (quadrupeds).
2. Build spine chain forward from pelvis (last spine bone before neck acts as chest).
3. Add neck + head.
4. Add tail from pelvis (`tail_01` = base near pelvis, numbers increase toward tip).
5. Build hind legs direct from pelvis (Keep Offset, head at hip socket).
6. Build front legs from chest via scapula (Keep Offset).
7. Add facial bones (jaw, ears, tongue chain under jaw).
8. Name consistently, Symmetrize `_r` → `_l`.
9. Assign bone groups (see §2.7).
10. Create shape keys for expressions bones cannot handle cleanly.
11. Weight paint / test deformation in Pose Mode.
12. Build Blender authoring IK (optional for posing; not exported).
13. Export deform skeleton only to UE 5.8.

**Pipeline split (locked):**  
- **Blender** = deform skeleton, weights, authored animation (including simple IK for posing).  
- **Unreal** = Control Rig / AnimBP for runtime foot plant, terrain adaptation, and later complex contacts (e.g. wyvern climbing).  
Export deform bones only.

Fully **manual** rigging.

#### 2.2 Core Hierarchy Philosophy

- **`root`** = non-deforming character origin on the **ground**, at the center of the four-foot stance (slightly forward of pure nose-to-tail midpoint is fine; not under hind legs only). Deform **off**.
- **`pelvis`** = deforming hip bone at pelvic center of mass. Child of `root` (Keep Offset). Parent of spine, tail, and hind legs.
- Hind legs attach directly to **pelvis** (pelvis is rigid in real life). Keep Offset; bone head at hip socket.
- Front legs attach to **chest** (last spine bone, e.g. `spine_03`) via dedicated **scapula** bones (shoulder blade is mobile). Keep Offset; scapula is **not** in the leg IK chain.
- Keep deformation bones close to the center of the volume they influence.
- Prefer slightly bent rest poses at major joints (knee, hock, elbow) for better IK and deformation later.

#### 2.3 Bone Orientation & Roll

- Always enable **Axes** while working. Roll number range (−180..180 vs 0..360) does not matter; axes do.
- Blender: **+Y** always along the bone (head → tail).

**Legs (both front and hind):**

| Local axis | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **+Y** | Along the bone | Length |
| **+X** | **Out** (away from midline on the authored side) | Hinge |
| **+Z** | **Forward** | Completes right-hand system |

- Hinge test: rotate **local X**. Front elbow folds **back**; hind knee folds **forward**.
- After **Symmetrize**, left-side +X often still points world-right. That is normal. Leave it if the fold test still passes.

**Centerline (spine / neck / head):**

| Local axis | Direction |
|---|---|
| **+Y** | Along the bone toward the head |
| **+X** | Character’s **right** |
| **+Z** | **Up** on a horizontal spine (more backward on a vertical neck is fine) |

**Tail:** +Y toward the tip (backward). Recalculate Roll Global +Z → +Z up, +X naturally **left**. Do not force tail +X to the right.

**Ears:** follow the ear geometry (+Y toward tip). Do not force world axes.

#### 2.4 Front vs Hind Legs

| Leg Type | Attachment | Intermediate? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hind | Pelvis | No | `hind_upper` head at hip socket |
| Front | Chest (`spine_03`) | Yes – scapula | Scapula → `fore_upper` → … |

**Scapula placement (general):**
- Head: withers height, just off the spine, slightly **behind** the chest–neck junction, in shoulder mass (not chest center of mass).
- Tail: shoulder joint (glenoid) on the **side** of the chest; bone runs **down, forward, and out**.
- Length: on the order of ~½–⅔ of the upper arm is fine.
- Motion: mostly small **translate** up/forward on stride; limited rotation. Limit Location + rotation as needed.
- Parent: chest, **Keep Offset**, not Connected. **Never** include scapula in the leg IK chain.

#### 2.5 Toes / Digits

- Low-poly / stylized: **1 bone per foot** is sufficient.

#### 2.6 Naming Convention (Locked)

- **Lowercase** throughout.
- Side suffix: **`_l` / `_r`** (not `.L` / `.R` — friendlier for Unreal).
- Numbered chains: `_01` = base / proximal, higher numbers toward the tip.
- Named only for one-off roles: `root`, `pelvis`, `scapula_r`, `jaw`, `head`.
- Limb prefixes for quadrupeds: **`fore_`** / **`hind_`** (not arm/leg anatomical names). Example: `fore_upper_r`, `hind_lower_l`.
- Scapula stays `scapula_r` (only one pair; no `fore_` needed).

**Symmetrize:** name the right side `*_r`, Edit Mode → Armature → Symmetrize. Do not scale X = −1.

#### 2.7 Bone Groups (Locked – use on all creature armatures)

Shared group set for Blender viewport organization and selection:

| Group | Contents |
|---|---|
| `AllBones` | Everything |
| `MiddleBones` | Midline (root, pelvis, spine, neck, head, jaw, tongue, tail) |
| `LeftBones` / `RightBones` | All sided bones |
| `ForeLeftBones` / `ForeRightBones` | Front limb chains (including scapula) |
| `HindLeftBones` / `HindRightBones` | Hind limb chains |

**Viewport colors (convention going forward):**
- Midline → **yellow**
- Front right → **red**
- Front left → **blue**
- Hind right → **pink**
- Hind left → **purple**

Rear (hind) groups use the distinct pink/purple so front vs back is readable at a glance.

#### 2.8 Blender Authoring IK (Posing Only)

Export does **not** include these controls. They exist to speed animation in Blender.

**Two-stage leg IK (digitigrade-friendly):**

1. **Leg IK** — constraint on `*_lower`, chain length **2**, target = `*_IK` (non-deform), pole = `*_pole`.
   - Front pole **behind** the elbow; hind pole **in front** of the knee.
   - Parent leg IK targets and main poles to **`root`** (Keep Offset).
2. **Paw IK** — constraint on `*_toes`, chain length **2**, target = `*_toes_IK` (non-deform).
   - **`*_toes_IK` is parented to the leg IK target** so the paw rides the plant by default and can be offset when needed.
   - Optional toes pole; if used, parent it to the toes IK (or leg IK). Often unnecessary on such a short chain.

- Uncheck **Deform** on all IK targets and poles.
- Do **not** also Copy Transforms the foot to the leg IK if the second IK owns foot + toes.
- Scapula, jaw, ears, tongue, tail, spine: **FK** (direct bone control + limits).
- Tail: for a first pass, key base / mid / tip rather than every bone.

**Limits:**
- Rotation limits on joints as needed.
- Location limits only on bones that translate (root, scapula, IK targets).
- On IK-chain bones, prefer the bone’s **IK limit** sliders over Limit Rotation constraints (constraints fight the solver).

### 3. Recommended Baseline Bone Counts

Starting points. Stylized creatures can lean lower; more expressive ones slightly higher.

| Section | Recommended | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Root + pelvis | 1 + 1 | Root non-deform ground; pelvis deform hips |
| Spine (torso) | Pelvis + 3 spine (last = chest) | e.g. `spine_01`–`spine_03` |
| Neck | 3 + Head | Good look-around range |
| Tail | 6–7 | 7 is smoother; 6 is leaner |
| Hind leg | `hind_upper` → `hind_lower` → `hind_foot` → `hind_toes` | From pelvis |
| Front leg | `scapula` → `fore_upper` → `fore_lower` → `fore_foot` → `fore_toes` | From chest |
| Ears | 3 per ear (`ear_01`–`ear_03`) | Base at skull, tip at point |
| Jaw | 1 | Lower jaw only |
| Tongue | 3–5 under jaw | `_01` = quiet attached base |

### 4. Facial & Expression System

#### 4.1 Bones

- **Jaw** — one lower jaw bone, parented to Head.
- **Ears** — `ear_01`–`ear_03` per side, parented to Head. Pivot at skull/ear-root mass (not deep in the cranium). For folded low-poly ears, run the chain along the structural fold, not in open cup air and not in one face only.
- **Tongue** — short chain parented to **Jaw** (not Head). `_01` sits in the attached rear third (limited motion so the mouth floor does not slide); higher numbers are the free length + tip. Neutral rest shape that works for pant / open mouth; tip curl shape keys optional later.

#### 4.2 Shape Keys (Morph Targets)

Preferred for skin bunching bones handle poorly (lips, eye open/close).

**Standard set:**

| Shape Key | Purpose | Notes / Values |
|---|---|---|
| Snarl | Raise upper lips, expose teeth + front gums | Basis = lips covering teeth slightly |
| Eyes | Multi-purpose eye state | 0.0 = closed, ~0.7 = normal, 1.0 = wide |

- **Snarl is independent of the jaw bone.** Animations may use both together (open mouth + snarl) or either alone. No driver from jaw rotation.
- No lip/muzzle bones — Snarl shape key covers lip raise / muzzle wrinkle.
- Eyes shape key driven in-game; combine with material work (below).
- Tongue shape keys: **optional later** only if the bone chain looks too tubular when panting.

**Topology rule:** Shape keys cannot change topology. Move vertices freely (including collapsing an eye quad); do not merge vertices.

#### 4.3 Material Interaction (Eyes)

- Eyes use a Dynamic Material Instance.
- Emissive on at night / in darkness (eyeshine).
- When Eyes shape key reaches **0.0** (fully closed), turn emissive off.
- Result: the glowing eye shape blinks closed.

#### 4.4 Optional / Future Shape Keys

- Separate left/right Snarl — low priority.
- MouthOpen / Roar morph for very wide opens — nice-to-have.
- TongueFlatten / tip curl — only if needed after animation tests.
- Extra eye/brow morphs — only if the face feels static after AI behaviors are in.

### 6. Full Animation Vision – Wolf (End Product)

Complete set for a living, readable wolf. Many entries are **polish-stage** and should not block foundation AI / locomotion.

#### 6.1 Core Locomotion (Foundation – do first)
- Idle (standing, weight shift, breathing)
- Walk
- Trot (primary travel gait)
- Run / canter or strong trot (one fast gait is enough at first)
- Turn-in-place / tight turns (or strong directional variants that work with movement)

#### 6.2 Combat & Threat (Foundation + early expansion)
- Alert / focus
- Growl / intimidate / threat display
- Bite / lunge (one solid montage minimum)
- Optional later: multiple attack variants, circle, dodge / sidestep, defensive snap
- Hit reaction / flinch
- Death (or clean transition into ragdoll)

#### 6.3 Ecological & Rest (High value, add soon after core)
- Sit (+ sit → stand)
- Lie down / rest (+ get-up)
- Howl (standing; sitting/lying variants later)
- Sniff (ground and/or air)
- Look around / head sweeps (can blend with dynamic Look-At)

#### 6.4 Flavor / “Alive” Idles (Polish)
- Scratch (ear, neck, side)
- Shake (wet / after combat / general)
- Stretch / yawn
- Panting (jaw + tongue bones; intensity from heat / exertion / state)
- Ear twitches, tail swishes, subtle weight shifts (additive or light procedural)
- Eat / chew / drink
- Mark / scrape (low priority)

#### 6.5 Social / Future Systems
- Play bow and related play animations (once pack or taming exists)
- Submission / passive postures
- Greeting / investigative mutual sniff
- **Taming / befriending related** (explicitly future): cautious approach, accept touch/food, relaxed companion idle, affiliative expressions

#### 6.6 Panting, Tongue, and Small Living Details

- Jaw bone opens the mouth; tongue **bone chain** provides visible tongue for pant / open-mouth states.
- Panting = looping or additive animation (slight jaw open + tongue motion + faster chest/spine breath).
- Drive intensity from runtime state (temperature, exertion, emotional state).
- Eyes blink stays independent of tongue/pant.
- Tail base sway / curl by state (alert vs relaxed vs aggressive).
- Occasional ear flicks.

#### 6.7 Priority Summary

| Stage | Focus | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | §6.1 + core of §6.2 + sit/lie/howl | AI loop testable and readable |
| Early expansion | Sniff, look-around, better combat variants | Stronger ecological + combat feel |
| Polish | Flavor set + panting/tongue + additives | Nearby creatures feel alive |
| Future systems | Taming / pack social set | Befriending and richer social AI |

### 8. Future Creature Workflow Checklist

1. Decide size class and locomotion type (quad, biped, flyer, etc.).
2. Apply baseline bone counts from §3, adjusting for style and anatomy.
3. Follow hierarchy, naming, axes, and bone groups from §2.
4. Add facial bones + shape keys only as needed (reuse Eyes multi-value pattern where possible).
5. Record species-specific decisions under `Creatures/`.
6. Decide which subset of the animation vision is required for that creature’s first playable version.
7. Test major deformation poses before heavy weight painting.
8. Author in Blender with optional IK; export deform bones only.
9. Update this document with any new general lessons learned.
