PLAYER:

Input: various controls will be configurable in settings with accessibility options (sticky activation, and others)
 - Q: activates Pickup/Drop/Place mode. While Q is held, looking at an equippable item and pressing LMB/RMB with that hand Unarmed picks it up — from the ground or from a station socket, same path. RMB = right hand, LMB = left (RMB-LMB flip toggleable in settings). If the hand is occupied and the player is looking at another item, the press drops the held item at the hand and picks up the target. A short ignore prevents the dropped actor from knocking the new held item or Unarmed. If the hand holds a real item, holding Q + that mouse button extends it with no swinging and preview-snaps to a nearby station socket (e.g. workbench slot). Drop/place commits on button RELEASE: near a valid socket → attach the existing actor at SocketTransform × PlacementOffsetTransform (on the item data asset); otherwise drop to the world. Held stacks always drop the entire stack on release and never place onto sockets (a stack exists only in a hand). More detail in the item system section.
 - LMB/RMB without Q: "activates" the held item causing it to enter an active state depending on the item. Weapons swing, containers enter take/put mode overriding the opposite hand's action, resources either do nothing or slightly extend for an examine action, or function as a makeshift weapon (swing a stick). When a hand holds a stack (count ≥ 2) primary actions are disabled.
 - R: Cancel action (ex: Cancel shooting arrow, just releasing button with drawn bow would shoot). Also used intentionally to start a stack: when both hands hold the same stackable item type, R + LMB/RMB calls into HeldItemsComponent to move one item into the clicked hand, creating a stack of 2.
 - Also basic controls: Sprint, Crouch, Inventory, and Jump.
 - E/F/other common keys: TBD, should be useful, but pickup is handled via extend + Q. I am sure as I add functionality I will find a good use for intuitive control. 
 - possibly LEFTALT to lock view direction?
Animations: mostly static poses with some short animations for pickup/drop and breathing animation. Most movement is caused by physics connection and dynamic movement. Each item should have a neutral and an extended pose. Also animations for crafting minigames, maybe others. Dual FPArms skeletal mesh components, one FPArmsAnimRef that is hidden and has zero IK/procedural and acts as the source of truth for what animation should be played and where the bones should be, and a second visible FPArms mesh that just copies the pose from AnimRef and applies Control Rig IK to the live item grip target from HeldItemsComponent (AnimRef Hand relative to WeaponBone × item world transform). AnimRef graph: per-arm Neutral/Extended plus LeftArm/RightArm slots, then a TwoHanded slot after the combine for Guided craft montages (Twist). Pipeline: Notes/ItemSystem/ItemAnimationPipeline.txt. Items do not use a grip socket. While an item is preview-snapped to a station socket in Q-mode, that arm switches to a shared palm-away ReleasePose and Control Rig follows the item loosely in location only (no rotation copy).
Physics based item holding: Items are connected to the WeaponBone for the corresponding hand via a Physics Control Component, tuned to feel controlable but react to player movement. The nice part of this is that the items would then also interact with trees/rocks, ground, other items, and creatures and bounce off/push them realistically. This allows for a REPO game style combat where the player attacks by swishing their screen around to smack things with the held tool. LEFTALT could allow the player to swing left and right while keeping an eye on the target maybe, to be seen when i implement these systems.
Hand ambivaence: The player should be able to hold items in either hand and the items will have identical functionality regardless. The default "handedness" will be right handed for any action that automatically sets an animation for both hands, but can be toggled in settings. For example, the player can hold a shield in left or right hand and it will block when holding the corresponding mouse button. The player could even hold two shields one in each hand if they wanted, although their actions may clash. The physics control would take care of it hopefully.
Survival considerations:
- Health - The player must manage their health, health when 0 player dies. Certain foods and items can restore health or cause a healing over time effect.
- Stamina - Stamina allows player to perform fast actions such as running or swinging items. The stamina will be a dynamic system where it fills constantly at a rate defined by the player's current state. Heavier items cause faster stamina drain when running or swinging. Certain buffs/debuffs can affect stamina regeneration. When stamina runs out, the player enters an out of breath state and the stamina regen is paused for a moment. 
- Temperature: If the player is too hot or too cold, they will gain a negative status effect. If the player is way too hot or way too cold they gain a worse status effect.
- Food and water: the player must eat calories to stay satiated. Hunger state may reduce stamina regen and possibly slow movement. Starvation causes further debuffs and may begin damaging the player's health.
- status effects: The player may gain certain states like wet, hot, cold, food illness, well-fed, etc. These states may apply buffs or debuffs, or damage type weaknesses or resistances depending on the status effect.

ITEM SYSTEM:

C++ based item system using Data Assets with C++ data fragments for item definiton, and ItemFactory for actually spawning the items in game.
ItemInstance holds current item data, for storing and retaining data such as durability and such.
Item hold alignment comes from the item origin, the item's Neutral/Extended HoldPose, and the dynamic grip transform on HeldItemsComponent. Hold is origin + Neutral/Extended HoldPose + dynamic grip. Left hand mirrors the item mesh (scale X=-1). Station actors (workbench slot, later tables / anvils) do use sockets; those sockets share one frame (origin on the surface, +Z up, +X forward). UItemDefinition carries PlacementOffsetTransform so an item sits correctly on any socket that follows that frame. Placing onto a socket is the Q + mouse hold (extend, no swing, preview snap) → release path; retrieving from a socket uses the same pickup path as a ground item. While preview-snapped, the arm uses a shared palm-away ReleasePose and only loose location IK (no grip rotation) so the wrist is not twisted to the socket.
- Resources: loose resources like sticks, rocks, small stones, fibrous plant material (harvested from fibrous plants), etc. Static harvestables including boulders, trees, ore veins, clay deposits etc that break into or produce resources that can be collected. Resources can be used as crafting ingredients and some as makeshift weapons or throwables.
- Weapons/Tools: basic tools, sharp edge allows chopping, cutting meat, slash damage. Tools are crafted from head and handle parts. more on crafting in crafting system section. Many tool types and tech levels (1: stone and bone, 2: flint and leather, 3: copper, 4: iron). Clay ware is T1-accessible if the player gathers and processes clay. Basic bowl is a hands craft; a large fire hardens it. Larger / nicer clay waits on a pottery wheel. A woven cordage basket can carry clay. First sharpenable tools: T3 copper at a grindstone (see CraftingSystemImplementation.txt). All tools should be usable as weapons with lower base damage. Specific weapon items have increased damage against creatures. Some tools/items will be blunt (stick, club, etc) where it can be swung and do full blunt damage in any direction. Some are one side bladed like axes. While swinging the player will try to align the blade, but the swing system should respect realistic arm twist limitations. Two sided bladed like a sword will align to the closest side when swinging. I plan to implement one handed shield items also which have a block action, raising in front of player to block attacks. Some weapons will be two handed. These items will have two different actions, one for each hand. Usually swing and block, but spear can stab/swing and throw for secondary. Bows/slingshots will have a "throw" which is shooting, and secondary weak block.
- Item Containers: containers are items that can store other items. The UI is as described above. Items have a size enum, S, SM, M, ML, L for storables. For example a bowl can store a bunch of S items but only a few SM items. A large pot can store a few L items but not much more. When the player is extending a container item, the other hand's extend action is overridden. On clicking with the other hand, the player will take the selected item or store one item depending if they are holding an item or not. Holding click for one second will take/store max. I will implement some way to add items from one container to another, and add items to containers on the ground as well. Some containers can hold liquids or particulates like sand or ash. Liquids and particulates stay in a container once added; they can be transferred to other valid containers. Once mixed, they cannot be separated. Solid items can still be added or removed. Items should be able to be dumped out on the ground with particulates and liquids dissapearing with some particle effects, maybe by holding R, the rest of the items should just spawn and drop to the ground. Containers are of significant importance to the cooking system, described below. 
- Clothing: TBD, need some way to equip clothing, may need an additional UI element for this. May add items such as belt which would allow storing additional tools or weapons on your person, make packs wearable, etc. Need to be able to equip and unequip bags, weapons and clothing as needed. May also want to add something to be able to equip two handed/long items to back. Bows should be able to be slung over the player's shoulder also. Belt loops and sheathes can be crafted to hold compatible items. Improvised cordage belt can hold one on each side, leather belt can hold two on either side. These can be cycled through, maybe using number buttons 1 cycles left hand, 2 cycles right hand, 3 cycles back storage slot into empty hand if possible, maybe automatically storing or swapping held item if possible. backpacks, bows can be stored on back. Other long handled tools can be stored on back with craftable weapon harness. Some items cannot be stored in some storage containers. Ash, wood shavings, and sand store in solid vessels (bowls, pots).

CRAFTING SYSTEM:
Implementation notes: Notes/CraftingSystem/CraftingSystemImplementation.txt
Milestone sequence: Notes/Roadmap.txt
Current task cards: Notes/Tasks.txt

The player will craft items using resources gathered from the world. While both hands are Neutral (not extended) and the held items (plus optional station slot) match one or more recipes, a quiet bottom-middle prompt shows `[E] Craft [Item]`. Mouse wheel cycles matches the same way as container lists. E starts the craft: view and movement lock, a Crafting input context takes over, and the mouse drives a minigame. R cancels. Any damage the player takes also cancels. Full interaction and recipe rules: Notes/CraftingSystem/CraftingSystemImplementation.txt.

Minigame *feel* is the destination: workpiece silhouette can change during the session (presentation SKM + morphs, not item meshes), and after the first checkpoint the raw item is replaced by a crafting-owned workpiece actor you can drop and resume (`[E] Resume Crafting: …`). Flip and knap/forge: later. Vision: Notes/CraftingSystem/CraftingSystemImplementation.txt §10.1, §11.4, §16. Core Loop A2 is grind motion on two live stones + Twist presentation; cancel returns both stones. Player holds items in A2; the craft SKM copies the workpiece item transform.
Some crafts will need a specific workstation such as a workbench, leatherworking bench, woodworking station, forge, or anvil. These stations will have one slot the player can place an item onto (Q + mouse hold to extend and preview-snap, commit on release) to enable 3 item type crafts, although not all workstation crafts will require three items. Retrieve the slotted item the same way as a ground pickup. Station slot accepts a single item.
Some crafts require multiple items of the same type, and so can use more than two or three items total but still less than three types. 
Some craftable items must be crafted as several recipes in a chain, which keeps any one recipe at three item types or fewer. One recipe may still run several minigame stages in a single session (Stone Hatchet Split / Seat / Wrap).
Some crafts require tools, tools must be held for these crafts, while other items may be in either hand or on the workstation slot. 
Examples of minigames:
- grinding two stones together to form sharpened stone (stone axe head), hand/ground craft.
- Flint knapping minigame - player holds hammer, holds flint opposite hand, at workbench, small circles appear over flint indicating how hard to slam hammer down to make progress toward craft. Similar for forging metal crafts, with tongs holding hot metal item.
- Twist cordage minigame, circle mouse left and right repeating.
- Stone Hatchet at the workbench is one recipe with three stages: strike-split, seat the head, wrap cordage.
Tags: Some resources or tools will be used for identical uses, so I'll implement gameplay tags to check if a held item has corresponding tags if it can be one of multiple items. Otherwise I'll check the item name. Some crafting recipe examples: Meat on a stick - any medium meat + stick, can be held extended over fire to cook. Copper sword - Long copper blade + short handle. Pot of lye - pot + water + ash, for preparing unprocessed hides for fleshing. Water can be collected by extending bowl or pot into a water source. wood shavings - Stick + any knife. Also produced from other crafts involving shaping wood. Bone armor plate - 2 Large Bones, 4 bone pieces, 2 cordage. Bone chest armor - 2 bone plates, 2 cordage.
Progression: the player will work their way up through the tiers of items. Most crafted tool components have a tier, handles, heads, and binding/string. There are short and long handles. Short and long blades, small and large axe heads, and others. Combining these creates tools or weapons. For example, Tier 1 short handle is a basic stick. Tier 2 is a carved handle from wood chunk (firewood piece). Tier 3 requires a cordage wrap grip. Tier 4 requires further processing (TBD, sealant or iron pins, idk yet). Heads require at least same tier - 1 handles. head tiers affect head durability and damage/effectiveness. Handle tiers affect handle durability. Heads and handles can break separately and can be recombined with repaired or replaced parts. For tools that are bound with cordage this destroys the cordage. Basic progression ramp: The player starts by crafting a sharpened stone from two stones. The player uses it to cut a tree. The player clears branches and chops tree into logs. use logs to craft basic workbench. Collect yucca leaves or bulrush stems, craft plant fibers, twist fibers to cordage. use workbench to craft stone axe (cordage, stick, sharpened stone). Use stone axe to chop logs into wood chunks. Craft wooden mallet (wood chunk, cordage, stick). Use wooden mallet for flint knapping. Craft flint knife (flint blade, cordage, short handle). Use flint blade to process animal carcass to retrieve tendons and unprocessed hide. (animals can be harvested for meat and bones with any sharp tool but requires knife for hide and tendons). Dry tendons, smash with blunt tool into fibers, twist into sinew cordage (Tier 2 cordage). Process hide (soak with ash+water in fired clay pot (2 clay), place on stretching frame and do de-fleshing minigame (scrape with knife). Soak defleshed hide in tanning solution (water+wood shavings boiled), staking post minigame results in processed hide (leather). Craft bellows with leather. Also can dry de-fleshed hide for rawhide as an ingredient for leather armor. Bellows used at crude bloomery furnace with charcoal and ore to smelt copper. Copper worked at forge + crude stone anvil (large stone, carried with two hands). Combine two copper ingots for copper billet. Copper billet craft to copper anvil. Stone anvil breaks, copper anvil lasts indefinitely for copper crafts. forge Copper pickaxe head, combine with T1 short handle (basic carved handle). Harvest iron ore with copper pickaxe. Smelt iron ore into iron bloom. Work bloom into ingot (craft at forge/anvil). Now repeat process to make iron anvil and now can make all iron tools. Crude bloomery furnace and charcoal pits break, later can make brick mold from wood plank with copper adze at woodworking station, can make brick charcoal pit and brick bloomery furnace which do not break. Each tier has armor sets, bone armor (2 bone plates (large bones, bone pieces, cordage)), leather armor (rawhide, leather, sinew cordage), copper plate armor, and iron plate armor. Each provides more protection than the last, enabling the player to face tougher enemies to retrieve better crafting materials. Crafting/progression notes: Recipe select is the pre-start prompt (wheel, container-style faded neighbors). The recipe locks when E starts the session. Knapping / forging may later allow reselect until 50 % progress as a minigame flag. For forging, the piece must be reheated if it grows too cold. If the player hits items too hard, a vertical bar will fill towards a broken item icon on the right of screen. If bar fills, craft fails, returns flint shards (arrowhead material) or 1-2 metal scrap (can be melted down to try again).

FOOD SYSTEM:

Dynamic food system with buffs and debuffs:
- Containers start in "General Mode" (can hold small items of appropriate size classes).
- Adding any food ingredient to an empty container locks it into "Food Mode"
- In Food Mode it only accepts further food ingredients.
- Non-food items already present block food from being added.
- Plate containers only ever accept food ingredients.
- Emptying a Food Mode container returns it to General Mode.
Food Formats:
- Raw Single
- Cooked Single (e.g. meat on a stick)
- Raw Mixed Dish (edible only if no raw meat is present)
- Cooked Mixed Dish
- Preserved (pickled, dried, etc.)
Edibility rules:
- Raw meat is edible but clearly marked dangerous (small skull and crossbones UI element maybe? - high chance of sickness).
- Any dish containing raw meat is unsafe until the meat is cooked.
- Some ingredients have different effects raw vs cooked.
Eating:
- Activate a food container + Hold E? = eat/drink 1 portion.
- Works for bowls, pots, plates, finished dishes, and valid raw plant mixtures.
- Solids can also be retrieved and eaten from the hand as normal.
- Vegetables/fruit can be eaten raw from the hand until clay containers exist.
Cooking & Transformation:
- Non-wooden bowls and plates and pots can be heated by placing on a fire or grate, thus cooking the ingredients/mixtures inside them.
- Successful cooking of only edible ingredients turns the container into a "Food Container" with stats derived from ingredients.
- Equipment progression: Clay (bowl, plate, jar, simple baking dish) → Copper (pan, pot) → Iron (improved versions).
Skewer / Small Animal crafting Flow:
- Live small animal + knife = kill.
- Dead small animal + knife = butcher → small unprepared animal skin + small cleaned carcass.
- small cleaned carcass + knife = several small meat peices.
- small cleaned carcass + stick (ground craft) = “X on a Stick”.
- Only medium whole meat cuts or small cleaned carcasses go on skewers.
Fermentation / Brewing: 
- Passive process in non-heated containers.
- Honey+water transforms into Mead; Fruit+water transforms into Wine; Honey + Fruit + water transforms into Flavored Mead.
- Procedural naming based on ingredients.
- From-scratch fermentation batches have a failure chance; adding starter (one portion with ladle or pour out from bowl) from a previous successful batch (or adding more raw ingredients to depleted batch, if not already turning to vinegar) guarantees success
- Further fermentation can produce vinegar (still named for what produced it?).
- Time scaled to the 24-minute day cycle (roughly 1 in-game day ≈ 4 real-world process days as a starting point).
Spoilage and Preservation:
- Items have spoilage tiers (Very Fast to Very Slow).
- Cooked food lasts longer than raw.
- While a large pot is kept hot, spoilage is reset
- container + vinegar + valid ingredient = pickling (long shelf life, reduced original effect + vinegar bonus).
- Drying/smoking for meat and some plants.
- Rotten food can be used as trap bait (optional mild “bad smell” effect).
Living Pot / Adding Ingredients Over Time:
- Explicit `CurrentPortions` counter (source of truth for how many portions remain).
- On eat: decrement portions, reduce all ingredients proportionally, delete any ingredient below a small threshold (~0.05–0.1).
- Eating does not recalculate name or effects.
- Adding a new ingredient immediately recalculates name + effects.
- After sufficient cooking time since the last ingredient addition, a second recalculation occurs.
- This allows a continuously maintained pot to evolve over time.
Effects & Satisfaction:
- Ingredients provide base effects (Warmth, strength?, Stamina buff, etc.).
- Raw consumption = weak version or different effect for some plant items.
- Being a prominent ingredient in a prepared dish applies a "Satisfaction Multiplier" (stronger/longer effect).
- Multiple different special effects in one dish dilute each other, encouraging focused recipes.
- Vinegar and certain preserved ingredients can act as enhancers, as well as various seasonings and spices that can be looted or crafted.
- Procedural dish names are generated from ingredients.


FIRE SYSTEM:

The player places a small fire blueprint with build menu button. The player can craft a bow drill and using that and a wood chunk can start the small fire after sticks and wood shavings are added. A small fire can be upgraded to a regular fire with firewood (wood chunks). A fire can be upgraded to a bonfire with logs. Each lasts significantly longer than the last and burns down in stages, and can be stoked unless fully burned out which takes some time. When fully burned out they leave ash and some charcoal. A bonfire nearly burned out, stoked with firewood, turns into a normal fire. A cooking rack can be built above a normal fire for cooking or a small fire for smoking. Items on a stick cook by holding the skewer extended over the fire. The same hold-over-fire transform may convert a wooden spear into a fire-hardened wooden spear. Other notes: Logs must have branches removed before they can be picked up (two hands) or used. All branches can be removed first or can remove per log depending on where the player hits the tree mesh. Logs can be chopped into wood chunks. structure Drying racks can turn smoked meat into jerky, defleshed hide into rawhide, tendons into dried tendons, etc.


CROP GROWTH:

Many crop seeds or cuttings can be propagated. Wild plants appear in various stages depending on season/time of year; seeding-stage plants yield many seeds.

Detailed design lives in:
- Notes/HarvestableSystem/HarvestableSystemDefinition.txt – high-level design (lazy Instance, crops/farming direction, yields, collision philosophy).
- Notes/HarvestableSystem/HarvestableSystemImplementation.txt – authoritative implementation plan (Factory API, fragment virtuals, Tree MVP, hybrid collision).

Summary of locked direction:
- Crops are Harvestables (Definition → Instance → Actor + fragments).
- Seeds = normal Size S pile Items (container-compatible, planting consumes uses; some later edible).
- Cuttings = larger Items that start the plant at an advanced stage + larger scale.
- MVP planting = direct on ground + per-crop MinPlantDistance check. Planter boxes later as Structures. Grid/hex snap can reuse structure blueprints later.
- Immediate tiny Sprout on plant. Stages: Sprout → Seedling → Growing → optional Flowering → Fruiting and/or Seeding. Smooth scale-up in early stages; mesh swaps at major boundaries.
- Growth = stage durations + continuous progress × environmental multipliers (season, soil sampled at plant time, water, health). Target ~15–20 in-game days for a carrot-like plant under normal conditions.
- Multi-harvest vs single-harvest supported. Yields are stage-specific (produce vs seed can shift).


CREATURES:

Categories:
- Ambient/tiny animals: tiny frogs, bugs, etc that jump or fly away when the player approaches. Fireflies in wetlands and wet clearings as cheap effects or instances; the Marshwisp Spider mimics those lights. Small ambient life should stay cheaper than full Actors.
- Small game: rabbits, birds, lizards, etc. Small creatures that produce small carcasses when killed and harvested. Rabbits can be captured and/or domesticated over time for a more reliable food source. Behavior and AI summary: The creature AI system is designed to create a living, reactive world where animals feel like distinct individuals within their species rather than generic enemies. Combat and encounters are intentionally multidimensional—risky and potentially lethal, yet usually avoidable through observation, preparation, stealth, or non-lethal interaction—so that engaging becomes a deliberate, high-reward choice rather than the default. Creatures assess the player and each other through a combination of personality, current state, size, and role, leading to believable decisions: a bold hungry wolf may commit to a fight that a cautious one would abandon, packs form and dissolve dynamically, and larger threats like the wyvern shift tactics based on injury and confidence. The overall intention is to reward player attention and agency while keeping every creature readable, species-true, and performance-friendly, producing emergent moments that feel grounded and memorable instead of scripted or one-dimensional.
